PACE vs. CARE-LG — What "0% CVR" Actually Means

Same mechanism you built, applied differently — the crux of the fairness question between these two methods.

How PACE gets to 0%

PACE (and this project's reimplementation of it) generates a candidate perturbation — a synthetic, optimized point — and runs it through an ASP/symbolic admissibility filter before accepting it as a valid counterfactual. If a candidate fails the constraint check, it's rejected and the search tries another. Only constraint-satisfying candidates are ever returned. So by construction, whatever comes out the other end has already passed the same kind of rule check CARE-LG's neurosymbolic layer does — that's why it's 0%, not a measured rate that could have come out otherwise.

Why this is real, but not the same claim as CARE-LG's

The difference isn't whether the constraints were checked — both systems genuinely check them. The difference is what's being checked against.

CARE-LG

The candidate is a real, recorded patient. When you verify "BP decreased, cholesterol decreased, sex unchanged," you're checking real, lived, physiologically-coherent values — a real human actually exists in that state.

PACE

The candidate is a synthetic, optimized point. It's checked feature-by-feature against the rules, and each rule passes — but nothing guarantees the combination is something a real body could actually be in. Every individual rule can pass while the combination is physiologically incoherent or unprecedented, because there's no real patient anchoring it.

This gap is very likely explained by the same real/synthetic distinction above: it's much easier to find some synthetic point that passes rule-by-rule checks than to find an actual real patient who satisfies all of them simultaneously. CARE-LG is solving a harder, more conservative problem — that's the honest reason for the lower number, not a weaker method.

The one-sentence fix for your slide

"Both CARE-LG and PACE achieve 0% CVR via rule-based filtering — but CARE-LG's targets are real, verified patients, while PACE's are synthetic points that satisfy the rules without a guarantee of real-world achievability."

That sentence is both accurate and still favorable to CARE-LG — it doesn't concede anything false, it just correctly scopes what "0%" means for each method.
